Life of a Donkey

The voice

One narrator, present tense, never hurried, faintly fond of the animal he is describing. He opens on “The classic Texas donkey.” and never once acknowledges that any of this is a joke — which is precisely what makes it one.

The narration also carries time, and that turned out to be the most valuable thing it does. His first night → Within a month → By spring → A year later. Four phrases, and the film stops reading as one bad session and starts reading as a life. Nothing else I tried solved that as cheaply.

The story in one pass

A donkey sits down at a $1/$2 table. The film treats him as a specimen and follows him through the five developmental stages every losing player passes through. He begins as a Fish who does not know which hands to fold, becomes a Calling Station who cannot fold at all, and matures into the Donk the film is named for. There he makes the worst play in the film, is called correctly by someone who knows better, and wins anyway on a 1.4% miracle. That single win destroys him: it turns him into a Maniac who shoves everything, and the Maniac busts down to a Nit — the adult form, and the saddest. Then the film pulls back: every chip he has lost has fed someone else at that table. He is the primary producer. The ring closes, and it begins again on Tuesday.

What still worries me

Three things I'd watch

The voice going smug. Seventeen lines is a lot, and the register only works while it stays clinical and slightly sad. he believes he is due and he will be back on Tuesday are each one degree from a wink. They're the best lines in the script and the first two I'd cut if the tone slips.

The silence at the river. The narrator says nothing across scene 11 — after twenty-nine seconds of talking, that gap is the loudest thing in the film. It only works if he has genuinely been present throughout; if the script thins out earlier, the silence stops registering.

Caption legibility at phone size. Lowercase, thin, letter-spaced type reads beautifully at full resolution and can vanish at 0.3 scale over a busy paper plate. Every caption needs a tested contrast treatment before I call any scene done.
